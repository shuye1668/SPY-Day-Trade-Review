A U.S.–Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz sank oil nearly 5%, removing the inflation and rate-hike fears that had shadowed the war and easing pressure on bond yields. Yet rather than broadening into the beaten-down cyclicals strategists urged, money rushed straight back into the AI memory and chip complex—Micron and Marvell proving this was a supercycle bet, not a real recovery rotation. Energy, banks, and defensive stocks paid for it. Leadership stayed alarmingly narrow: the Nasdaq rose triple the Dow while small caps barely budged, leaving a sentiment-led rally above softening factory data. Durability now hinges on Hormuz opening and Micron's June 24 results.
