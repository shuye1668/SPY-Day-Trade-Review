Trump declared the Iran ceasefire effectively over, and US strikes sent Brent above $80—instantly reviving inflation fears. Money markets flipped from pricing rate cuts to betting the Fed could hike by October, and a hawkish June FOMC minutes (some officials wanted hikes) sealed it. Yields climbed to 4.58%, punishing anything rate-sensitive or leveraged: consumer-credit names and homebuilders were dumped, while energy refiners surged on soaring oil. Chipmakers bounced hard, pushing Nasdaq green even as the Dow fell 1.1%. The split tells the story—this was rotation, not panic. But defensive staples failed to shelter, hinting at indiscriminate deleveraging beneath the surface.
