August payrolls came in at 162,000 — three times what economists expected — and traders lifted the odds of a Fed rate hike this month to roughly 60%. Short-term yields jumped, but ten-year yields barely moved. Because long-term borrowing costs stayed put, investors did not flee risky assets; they switched sides inside the AI trade. After Broadcom and Dell revealed enormous orders and warned that memory chips, not accelerators, are the real shortage, money left the companies paying for AI — Microsoft, Apple, Alphabet — and moved into the suppliers selling the scarce parts. Chip stocks rose 3.4% while the S&P 500 fell.
