Houthi strikes on Saudi energy facilities and US attacks near Kharg Island drove Brent to $99 intraday, arriving three days before August CPI and one week before a Fed meeting markets now price above 50% for a hike. Gold fell 0.84% and Treasuries found no bid — this was repriced as a discount-rate event, not a war scare. Capital rotated out of anything valued on distant cash flows or exposed to fuel costs, and into assets with current-quarter pricing power: Intel's announced price increases, Seagate's sold-out capacity, Corning's signed fiber contract. Semiconductors rose 1.30% while the Dow lost 1.18%.
