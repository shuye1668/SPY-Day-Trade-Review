September added just 29,000 jobs and pay barely rose, which eased fears of another Fed rate hike but did not bring interest rates down. Unemployment ticked up mainly because more people started looking for work, not because companies cut staff. Oil also bounced back after a G7 plan to release emergency diesel, so the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.28%, near its highest since 2002. Stocks still gained and the Nasdaq 100 hit a record, as investors bought companies that profit from shipping more AI hardware, such as chip testers. Storage makers fell instead: Toshiba's plan to double hard-drive output sent Seagate down 10%.
