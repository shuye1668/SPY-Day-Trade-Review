The Federal Reserve, under new Chair Kevin Warsh, held interest rates steady but signaled it may raise them this year—nine officials now expect at least one 2026 hike. That hawkish surprise pushed short-term yields up sharply and sent stocks lower. Investors sold expensive software and high-growth tech, where rising rates hurt future earnings most, and rotated into AI hardware—chips, memory, and data-center power—the lone group to close higher. Small caps fell least, showing the selling was concentrated in big-cap growth rather than broad panic. From here, upcoming inflation data, more than the Fed itself, will steer the market.
