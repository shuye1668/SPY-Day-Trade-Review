A weak June jobs report and easing service-sector cost pressures — reinforced by oil sliding to multi-month lows after Saudi Arabia's deepest price cut in decades — convinced markets the Fed is done hiking and may cut in September. That relief, plus Nvidia's assurance its product roadmap is on track, erased last week's panic that AI spending was cooling. Investors sold the safe, defensive stocks they had crowded into and bought back beaten-down AI hardware and chipmakers. Notably, short-term bonds rose alongside stocks — a sign this was about lower-rate relief, not fear. The rally's staying power hinges on upcoming big-tech spending plans.
