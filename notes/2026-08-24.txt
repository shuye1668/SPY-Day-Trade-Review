Nvidia told big customers that AI server prices will jump over 15% next year, blamed mostly on soaring memory costs—and hours later, Taiwan prosecutors charged an Nvidia manager and two Supermicro staff in a chip-smuggling case. Chipmakers cratered: memory, storage, and optical names fell 5–8%, dragging the Nasdaq down 1%. But this wasn't a market crash—it was a rotation. The Dow rose, banks and gold gained, and AI software winners like Microsoft and Meta climbed. Money left expensive AI hardware, not the market. Everything now hinges on Wednesday's Nvidia earnings and inflation data to prove demand still holds.
