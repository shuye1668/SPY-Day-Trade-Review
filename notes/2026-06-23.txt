Forced selling of leveraged Korean memory-chip ETFs triggered a 10% Kospi crash that spread straight into US technology, hitting the most expensive, most crowded AI names hardest — the chip index fell roughly 8%. Stretched valuations after a near-doubling had left these stocks fragile; the Korean unwind was just the spark. Hot PMI data and a hawkish BofA note revived "higher-for-longer" rate fears, accelerating the move. Yet investors rotated into defensives rather than dumping equities wholesale: Treasuries drew only modest haven buying, credit stayed wide open, the Dow held flat. A positioning unwind, not panic. Micron's results test whether AI-spending demand still justifies the cost.
