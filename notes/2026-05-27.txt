Mixed signals on Iran set the tone: oil dropped sharply on hopes of a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz — but the US launched new airstrikes and Trump said he wasn't satisfied, so stocks stalled at record highs instead of climbing. Weak data (mortgage applications −8.5%, a shrinking Richmond Fed survey) pushed the 10-year yield down to 4.48%, sending money into safe dividend stocks like P&G, while banks fell on JPMorgan's rising costs. Underneath, the gains came from just a few AI giants (Micron, Meta, Amazon); crowded software (Zscaler −32%) and chips were sold off. Bottom line: a narrow rally built on hope, not strength.
