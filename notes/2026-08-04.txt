Hopes for a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz sent oil tumbling below $80, and cheaper energy eased inflation fears, so Treasury yields fell. At the same time, strong big-tech earnings—and a big upgrade to how much these companies plan to spend on AI—reassured investors that the spending is paying off, the very fear that had sunk stocks on July 23. Both worries lifted at once. Money flowed back into growth and beaten-down chip stocks, and even the bond market showed less stress. The S&P 500 and Dow closed at record highs, though Friday's jobs report and an unsigned Hormuz deal still loom.
