Thirty-year Treasury yields blew past 5.19%—the highest in 19 years—and forced investors to rethink everything. Hot CPI, stubborn PPI, and oil above $100 brought Fed rate-hike talk back to the table. Money fled expensive growth stocks and piled into things that hold up when inflation bites: energy names like EQT, drugmakers like Eli Lilly, and grocers like Kroger and Coca-Cola. Homebuilders, solar, and ad-tech took the worst hits. Chips split in two—AI connectivity winners like Astera Labs and Marvell jumped on analyst upgrades, even as BofA warned the crowded semi trade is close to flashing a sell signal. With Nvidia's earnings next, the AI story has to prove it can survive a yield shock it simply can't outrun.
