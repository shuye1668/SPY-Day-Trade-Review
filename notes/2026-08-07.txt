July payrolls unexpectedly fell by 23,000—the first drop since February—and traders cheered it, betting a cooling job market lets the Fed skip a rate hike. Short-term yields and the dollar dropped, so high-growth tech rallied and the S&P 500 closed at a record, capping its best week since April. Underneath, a quieter split ran through AI: optical-connection makers (Coherent, Lumentum, Marvell) surged on data-center demand, while memory names (Micron, Seagate) sagged after Citi warned prices may peak by mid-2026. The catch: this rally treats weak jobs as "rate relief," not a growth warning. Tuesday's inflation report is the real test.
