American forces struck Iran near the Strait of Hormuz, Iran hit back, and oil jumped above $90. Normally war headlines send investors into safe bonds — but this time bonds fell too, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest since early 2025. That flipped the story: pricier oil means more inflation, and Fed Chair Warsh just signaled he may raise rates in September. So investors dumped expensive, high-growth tech and software and bought energy and steady consumer names. Stocks barely moved overall, yet underneath, California utilities crashed on a wildfire-liability ruling and AI-lending worries quietly surfaced in credit markets.
