Japan's suspected currency intervention pushed the yen up 2.1% against the dollar, which took pressure off US Treasuries — Japanese investors had been selling them to hedge. Then Fed governor Waller said he could leave rates alone in September, cutting hike odds to a coin flip. Money chased whatever benefits most from cheaper money: crypto-linked stocks and expensive software. Chipmakers were left behind after Broadcom warned that building AI chips is squeezing its profit margins. The catch: bonds gave back almost all their gains by the close, while stocks kept theirs. The August inflation report on September 11 decides who was right.
