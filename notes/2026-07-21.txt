Hedge funds had already dumped their chip and momentum positions at a record pace — so by Tuesday there was almost nobody left to sell. Semiconductors jumped 5.2%, led by memory names Micron and Seagate on rising DRAM contract prices, even though oil climbed toward $92 and bond yields hit new highs. Normally that combination hurts stocks; it didn't, because this rally was about traders rebuilding positions, not about the economy improving. Money moved sideways — out of cloud software and Chinese internet, into beaten-down hardware. Strip out chips and the Nasdaq 100 actually fell. Tesla and Alphabet earnings decide what happens next.
