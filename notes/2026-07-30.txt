The Fed held rates but scrapped its usual roadmap for what comes next, and Chair Warsh kept stressing the fight against inflation. With no guidance to anchor them, and with oil near $90 and a hot growth-price reading, bond investors demanded more compensation—30-year yields hit their highest since 2007. That crushed expensive tech and chips, sending the Nasdaq 100 into a correction. After the close, the mood split: Microsoft's cloud surge proved AI spending is paying off and sparked a huge chip rebound, while Meta's soft outlook got punished. This wasn't panic selling—it was money rewarding proven AI winners.
