Saudi Arabia found a way around the Hormuz bottleneck — half its damaged pipeline back within days, crude shuttled by ship outside the strait — and Brent fell a second day to $104.82. With the oil shock looking bounded, traders stopped pricing an endless Fed hiking path: 10-year yields slid 9bp to 4.93%, and money rushed back into the longest-duration, most beaten-down chips. But the gain was made before New York opened, equipment makers barely moved, small caps and the Dow hardly joined, and anyone needing to borrow was punished — CoreWeave fell 4.2% after a $3bn convertible. Cheaper discount rates, not better earnings.
