A $70 billion five-year Treasury auction failed badly — dealers were stuck with $11 billion nobody wanted, hours after September business-activity data hit a five-year high. Ten-year yields jumped 14 basis points to 5.11%, the highest since 2007. Money got more expensive, so investors sold whatever depends on cheap money: small caps fell hardest, then chips, then big tech, with the Dow least hurt. Gold fell too — proof this was a rate shock, not a growth scare. Credit insurance on Oracle and SoftBank kept widening while banks stayed calm. The bill for AI capex, not AI demand, is being repriced.
