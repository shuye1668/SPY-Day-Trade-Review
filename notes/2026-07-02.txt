A sharply weaker June jobs report — 57,000 payrolls versus expectations near 113,000, compounded by 74,000 in prior-month cuts — took the pressure off the Fed to raise rates. Falling short-term yields pushed capital out of crowded AI-hardware trades and into steadier value and defensive names, with healthcare leading. Fresh doubts about whether AI spending is justified — signals of chip oversupply and cheaper computing costs — deepened the semiconductor selloff. The divide was stark: the Dow hit a record on defensive buying while the S&P closed flat, with all the damage concentrated in chips. What follows depends on whether upcoming tech earnings can rebuild confidence in AI demand.
