New York Fed's John Williams said there's no clear sign a September rate hike is needed, and called high bond yields a sign of a strong economy, not runaway inflation. With oil's rally also cooling, stocks snapped a three-day slide. Yet only short-term US yields fell; German and Japanese yields kept rising, so just one Fed meeting got cheaper, not the inflation problem. Investors sold expensive software and bought steel, banks and payments. Nvidia, on an unconfirmed takeover rumour, supplied about half the S&P's gain. Dell, Palo Alto Networks and Credo each blamed memory and energy costs for shrinking margins. Friday's jobs report decides.
