Brent crude jumped 6% to $107.6 after the heaviest attacks on shipping since the Iran war began, reviving inflation fears. Traders now see a 70% chance the Fed raises rates next week, and a smaller-than-expected Treasury bond buyback pushed the 10-year yield to 4.95%, its highest since 2023. Higher rates hit the priciest, most popular stocks hardest: chipmakers fell 2.7% as investors dumped AI-hardware winners. The S&P 500 slipped 0.6%, its fourth straight loss, cushioned by Apple's 3.6% gain. Gold fell and credit markets stayed calm, signaling a rate scare, not panic. Friday's inflation report decides what comes next.
