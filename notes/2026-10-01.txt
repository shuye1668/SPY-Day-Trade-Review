Fed Vice Chair Jefferson said officials need "more time" before raising rates again, and traders cut the odds of an October hike from about 70% to 24%. That pulled the 10-year Treasury yield down from 5.34%, its highest since 2002, to 5.24%, and the S&P 500 recovered an early loss to close up 0.2%. But 30-year yields barely moved, oil jumped 3%, and factory input costs rose, so inflation worries remain. Investors sold healthcare and media stocks and bought tech consulting and chip suppliers after upbeat updates from Accenture and Micron (ACN, MU). Friday's jobs report is the next test.
