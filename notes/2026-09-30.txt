Cooler August inflation, revised even lower, pushed the odds of an October Fed hike below 40%. But the same morning showed an economy still running hot: second-quarter growth was revised up and a Chicago factory gauge jumped to nearly 59. So short-term yields stayed calm while long-term yields kept climbing, with the 30-year near its highest since 2002. That hurt stocks that behave like bonds, such as food makers, power producers and Dow heavyweights, while cash-rich tech and AI software held up, led by Apple and Synopsys. Quarter-end rebalancing then erased the gains in the last half hour.
