Software stocks crashed even as Oracle and Adobe beat earnings—investors now fear AI agents will replace subscription software, so a strong quarter no longer matters. The money didn't leave AI; it moved to the companies that build it. Oracle's massive AI spending plan actually lifted chip-equipment and memory makers, sparking a semiconductor surge. Meanwhile, Trump signaled an Iran deal, sending oil and bond yields lower and boosting risk appetite, which spread the rally into industrials and small caps. Indexes closed higher, but leadership flipped: software is now cheap, chipmakers lead, and AI is no longer treated as one single trade.
