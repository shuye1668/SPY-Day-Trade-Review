Washington pulled Iran's oil-sale waiver after tankers were struck in the Strait of Hormuz, and that single move rippled across every market. Crude jumped over 5%, and fears that pricier energy means stickier inflation pushed the 10-year yield past 4.55%. Higher rates hit the most expensive stocks hardest, so investors dumped chipmakers—the year's biggest winners—sending the SOX down 4.65% with only Nvidia surviving. The cash didn't leave the market; it rotated into oil names, steady defensive stocks, and software that doesn't rely on chips. Most S&P 500 stocks actually rose. This was a reshuffle, not a retreat—next week's tech earnings decide what's next.
