Micron's blowout guidance and take-or-pay contracts reclassified the memory cycle as a structural AI super-cycle, not a transient spike. That repricing forced a two-way split: capital migrated into the memory supply chain while fleeing the cost-bearing hardware OEMs, with Apple's price hikes proving the margin-tax thesis. Sticky Core PCE and resilient spending simultaneously hardened a higher-for-longer regime, compressing high-multiple software. The net result was a concentration unwind—Magnificent Seven down, equal-weight and defensives bid—masked by a flat headline index. Even NVIDIA became a funding source as leadership rotated from compute to memory. Breadth is widening beneath the surface; the forward risk is whether megacap erosion eventually overwhelms the rotation.
