Trump's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz sent Brent above $83, and Fed Governor Waller responded by saying rates may need to rise. That turned an oil story into a rate story — traders now see a coin-flip chance of a July hike, and 10-year yields jumped to 4.62%. Stocks and bonds fell together, so bonds offered no shelter. Investors dumped expensive, capital-hungry AI hardware and bought cheap cash-flow businesses and energy instead. Look at the gap: chips fell 4.78%, the Dow just 0.26%. They sold on valuation, not fundamentals — TSMC grew sales 36% and still dropped. Oil now sets the discount rate.
