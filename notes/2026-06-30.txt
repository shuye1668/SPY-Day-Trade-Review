Strong jobs and services data—better-than-expected JOLTS openings and a rebounding Dallas Fed services reading—signaled the economy isn't slowing, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield up about ten basis points to 4.46%. Higher rates hurt income-style stocks, so money fled bond-substitute defensives—REITs, telecom, staples—into semiconductors and AI-infrastructure names, powered by a memory-chip shortage and broad analyst upgrades. That handoff capped the best quarter for chips on record. The result is a narrow, momentum-led rally concentrated in expensive tech: those valuations now hinge on second-quarter earnings proving AI spending becomes revenue, with rising yields the biggest risk to leadership.
