The U.S. Treasury surprised markets before the open by pledging to at least double its purchases of long-term government bonds, stepping in after 30-year yields hit their highest since 2007 and national debt crossed $40 trillion. That pushed the 30-year yield down about 10 basis points to 5.18% and lifted most stocks. As borrowing costs eased, money left the year's most crowded trade—AI chips and hardware, where Broadcom and Seagate fell hard—and moved into smaller companies, consumer names, and healthcare, where Moderna's cancer-vaccine success sparked a 177% jump. This was calm rotation, not panic: the VIX fell 6%. But a weak bond auction warns the fix may be temporary.
