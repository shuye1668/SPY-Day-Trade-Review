TSMC posted strong results but raised its spending forecast—and investors read that as a warning, not good news. If the chip leader has to spend even more, maybe the AI boom isn't paying off fast enough. Chip stocks dropped 4.3%, and money rushed out of expensive AI names into steady, profitable companies with solid earnings like Abbott and Merck. Rental prices for AI computing chips are falling too, adding real evidence to the "overbuilding" worry. Hawkish Fed comments and higher oil from the Iran conflict didn't help. But small-caps held flat—this was money rotating, not fleeing. Chip leadership is now on watch.
